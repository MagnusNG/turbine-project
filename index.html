The Problem

Wind doesn't arrive on a schedule — grids need power that does.

WEGEN's Vertical-Axis Wind Turbine spins an alternator whose output voltage and frequency shift constantly with wind speed — anywhere from 50V to 500V AC, three-phase. A home or utility grid can't accept that directly; both expect a steady 240V at 60Hz.

  • Serves a gap in affordable small-scale wind conversion for rural and off-grid users
  • Displaces fossil-fuel generation, aligned with the EGBC's public and environmental welfare principle
  • Requires power electronics that can regulate, protect, and invert a genuinely unpredictable input
Design Objectives

• Design a 10kW grid-tie system converting alternator output to usable AC power

• Produce a single-line diagram (SLD) of the full electrical system

• Simulate performance under varying wind and voltage conditions

• Estimate prototype cost from a validated components list

• Build a reduced-scale demo model to validate the architecture

System Architecture

Rectify, regulate, then invert.

Informed by a review of small-wind conversion literature (Blaabjerg et al.; Shen & Izadian; Ramos-Paja et al.), this rectifier → DC bus → DC/DC → inverter architecture was selected over a direct AC/AC (matrix converter) design, which lacks a convenient DC bus for battery storage or dump-load protection.

01

Diode Rectifier

Converts the alternator's variable 3-phase AC to DC. Literature confirms a simple uncontrolled rectifier is sufficient here.

02

Regulated DC Bus

A stable node for downstream conversion, with headroom for future battery storage (BESS) integration.

03

DC/DC Regulator

Boost or buck stage that irons out the wide voltage swing before inversion.

04

Grid-Tie Inverter

Delivers stable 240V, 60Hz AC to a home or the utility grid, with a dump load protecting against excess power.

Small-Scale Demo Model

Proving the architecture on a desktop.

A hand-built, low-power stand-in for the full 10kW system — a 3D-printed turbine spun by a desk fan, driving a stepper-motor alternator through the same rectify-regulate architecture at a fraction of the scale.

Demo Circuit

Stepper-motor alternator → bridge rectifiers → zener-clamped DC bus → DC/DC boost → LED load. A reduced-power analog of the full architecture above.

Turbine Rotor — Savonius Design

Curved-scoop blades were chosen over flat panels for omnidirectional self-starting — the rotor spins from wind in any direction without needing to face into it.
